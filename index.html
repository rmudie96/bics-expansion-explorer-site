      The previous full-industry baseline has been replaced with the government’s 2027/28 BICS forecast. Expansion benefits within industries already listed are now included. These figures are a quantified subtotal: some additional nuclear, utility and extraction consumption remains unestimated.

      Model documentation

      Methodology

      The model uses published national electricity statistics and local employment and economic data to estimate the additional relief available under each expansion. It assumes £40/MWh, automatic full take-up and no minimum consumption threshold in the expansion. The baseline uses the government’s actual-policy forecast.

      The methodology note explains the calculation, the differences between the three allocation methods, and the assumptions and limitations that affect the results.

      Read the methodology →
      Key limitations

      Modelled existing support. Current BICS uses the published 2027/28 forecast, allocated within government industry bounds. Expansion can benefit additional consumption within listed industries. Industry shares of existing support remain estimates; no individual product or certificate register is modelled.

      Estimated geography. Local electricity use is allocated from employment and regional economic data. It is not a reading of constituency meters. High agreement between methods does not prove accuracy.

      Conditional industry inputs. Activities requiring unverified sector shares, including electricity and gas utilities, are not quantified. Other industry quantities use published inputs with documented allocation or expenditure proxies. These are model estimates. No site-consumption distribution or small-site exclusion is used.

      Financial potential only. Results are recurring full-year savings at £40/MWh and automatic uptake. They do not forecast claims, jobs, investment, productivity or economic growth. Local allocation ranges are not confidence intervals or a complete uncertainty range.
